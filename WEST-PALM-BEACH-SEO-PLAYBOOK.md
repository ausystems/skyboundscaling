# West Palm Beach SEO Playbook: Skybound Scaling

The operating document for the Palm Beach County ecosystem built into this site.
BUILT = implemented in the codebase. OPS = operational work outside the code.

Positioning (used across the cluster): We help West Palm Beach and Palm Beach County
businesses attract more customers, generate qualified leads, and scale revenue through
websites, SEO, paid advertising, and automation.

Rules enforced: every page unique, no city-name-swap pages, no doorway pages, no em
dashes, no ranking guarantees, every page has one search intent and one conversion
goal, local context in service of a point (never stuffed).

## 2. Why this structure and not 17 cities x 6 pages

The brief named 17 target locations. Building six service pages for every one of
them (102 pages) would violate the brief's own quality rules: pages for Highland
Beach or Palm Beach Shores cannot contain unique local business substance, and
Google's spam policies treat those as doorway pages. Doorway penalties would sink
the exact rankings this project exists to win.

The structure that ranks instead:

- Tier 1 (BUILT): full West Palm Beach cluster carrying every county-level
  commercial keyword, including all ten SEO targets from the brief.
- Tier 2 (BUILT): deep, unique hubs for the six markets with real economies:
  Boca Raton, Delray Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, Jupiter, Wellington, Boynton Beach.
- Tier 3 (coverage without doorways): Lake Worth Beach, Royal Palm Beach,
  Greenacres, Riviera Beach, North Palm Beach, Lake Park, Lantana, Tequesta,
  Palm Beach Shores, Highland Beach are served through the county hub's areas
  section, city-cluster blog posts (topics 97 to 100 below), and campaign geo
  targeting. GBP service areas cover all 17. OPS
- Expansion trigger: promote a Tier 3 town to its own page only when there is
  unique substance to publish: completed local projects, local reviews, photos,
  and a distinct economic story. Same rule applies before adding per-city service
  pages like /locations/boca-raton/seo/: each must pass the "could this exist for
  another city?" test with a no.

## 3. Internal linking map (BUILT)

- Locations index: cards for WPB and all six county guides; aside links every WPB
  service page.
- WPB hub: links all 6 WPB service pages, 3 WPB industry pages, all 6 county
  guides, the South Florida industry playbooks for trades, both county blog posts.
- WPB service pages: link the hub, siblings, the global /services/ counterpart,
  county guides where relevant, and the county blog posts.
- County city guides: link the WPB hub, 4 to 5 WPB service pages with city-specific
  framing, neighbor city guides, and the county local SEO guide.
- Blog posts: money page in the first third, contact CTA at the end.
- Anchors are descriptive: "West Palm Beach Google Ads management", "SEO West Palm
  Beach", "Wellington marketing guide".

## 5. Technical SEO (BUILT unless OPS)

Identical standards to the Miami cluster: clean canonical trailing-slash URLs,
Organization + Service + FAQPage + BreadcrumbList + ItemList schema validated on
every page, Open Graph and Twitter cards, en-US locale, one h1, root-absolute
internal links, sitemap.xml regenerated with the full cluster, robots.txt pointing
to it. Host-level items (compression, caching, redirects, security headers) and
Search Console submission are OPS per the README launch checklist.

## 6. Local SEO operations (OPS)

1. GBP strategy: the West Palm Beach listing (or the single listing's service
   areas) must cover all 17 target municipalities. Categories: Marketing agency,
   Internet marketing service, Website designer. Link the profile to
   /locations/west-palm-beach/ with UTM tags.
2. Reviews: harvest hardest November through April when client volume peaks;
   respond year-round; never buy or gate.
3. Citations: consistent NAP across Google, Apple, Bing, Yelp, Clutch, UpCity,
   chambers (West Palm Beach, Boca, Delray, Palm Beach North).
4. No street address is published by design; when one exists, add it to schema,
   footer, and GBP together.

## 7. Seasonal operating calendar (the county's real SEO lever)

- May to September: technical SEO, content production, review responses, site
  improvements. Build before the buyers land.
- October: everything live, GBP polished, tracking verified, budgets staged.
- November to April: paid campaigns at full seasonal budgets, review harvesting,
  weekly optimization. Trades add a June to November storm-season overlay.

## 8. What we deliberately did NOT do

- No 17x6 doorway matrix. See section 2 for the expansion trigger that adds pages
  the moment they can be genuinely unique.
- No invented local claims, client names, or review counts.
- No ranking guarantees anywhere. "Positioned to become the number one choice" is
  a strategy statement, never a promise on a page.

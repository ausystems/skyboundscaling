The problem

In Miami, wasted ad spend is not a rounding error. It is the difference between an account that prints jobs and one that quietly bleeds.

Miami buys its clicks at some of the steepest prices in the country. Injury and immigration keywords trade at triple digits, which is why Miami law firms treat paid search like a capital investment. Home services are not far behind: when a storm system forms in the Atlantic, every roofer in Miami-Dade bids on the same panicked searches at once, and costs spike exactly when the demand does. At these prices there is no such thing as a harmless mistake. Every broad keyword, every unqualified click, every untracked call is real money gone.

Owners usually reach us in one of two states. Burned: a year of agency invoices, a dashboard full of impressions, and no idea what any of it actually booked. Or bleeding: self-managed campaigns where Google's automated recommendations quietly widened the targeting and spent the budget teaching the algorithm instead of booking customers. Both get the same treatment, an account rebuilt around one question: which searches turn into revenue, and what are we paying for them?

The waste we cut first

04 leaks

01

Broad match leaking into the wrong Miami

Broad match in a tourist city is a shredder. A plumber's budget ends up on vacation rental questions, a firm's on paralegal job searches, a med spa's on people browsing treatments from a beach chair with no plan to book. We rebuild match types and keep the query reports on a leash, so ads show for buyers, not browsers.

02

No negative keyword discipline

Free, salary, DIY, school, careers: the queries that never buy still spend. We maintain negative lists per campaign and review search terms weekly, because in a market priced like Miami, a week of leakage can fund a competitor's whole month.

03

Ads pointed at a slow homepage

Clicks this expensive deserve better than a five-second load and a homepage about everything you do. We send every ad to a fast page that repeats its exact promise, one service, one area, one action, then tune those pages with Miami conversion rate optimization until the click math works.

04

Nobody knows what booked

No call tracking, no form attribution, no CRM connection, just a feeling that the ads are probably working. We wire call tracking numbers, form events, and CRM revenue back into the account so bids chase booked jobs instead of raw clicks. It is the same plumbing behind our Miami lead generation systems.

Geo strategy

A campaign that treats Miami-Dade as one circle on a map is already losing. Miami Beach intent and Kendall intent are different economies.

The searcher on Collins Avenue looking for an emergency dentist is a visitor who needs help within walking distance today and will never book again. The Kendall homeowner searching for AC replacement is choosing a company to trust for the next decade. Same keyword family, completely different value, and they should never share a bid. Doral splits again: the logistics operator by the airport searching for commercial service has nothing in common with a Doral Isles household, so commercial and residential run as separate campaigns with separate copy.

So we build geography into the account itself: neighborhood and radius targeting around where your real customers are, bid adjustments by area value, tourist zones separated from residential zones or excluded outright. Language gets the same respect. Roughly seven in ten Miami-Dade households speak a language other than English at home, so Spanish campaigns are parallel builds with their own keyword research, their own ad copy, and their own landing pages. Translated English does not convert here. Campaigns conceived in Spanish do.

Budget honesty

Small budgets are not doomed in Miami. They just have no room for waste, and no business pretending to be everywhere.

A focused $2,500 in monthly spend can often win a specific service in a specific slice of the county: implant consults in Coral Gables, drain clearing in Kendall, one offer, one geography, ruthlessly negative-matched. What it cannot do is compete county-wide in legal or storm restoration, where a single afternoon of triple-digit clicks would drain it. We would rather tell you that on the first call than learn it with your money. Our Miami Google Ads budget guide breaks down what different spend levels actually buy here.

And paid search is not always the answer. When a category rewards patience more than speed, we say so and point the budget at rankings instead; the tradeoffs live in our SEO vs Google Ads comparison. If you want the honest read on your market, book a call and bring your numbers. We will tell you what we would do with them, including the parts that do not involve paying us.

Miami PPC questions, answered

What budget do Google Ads need in Miami?

Enough to buy meaningful data in your category. For most Miami home services that typically means $2,000 to $4,000 per month in ad spend before management. Legal and other triple-digit-click markets usually need $5,000 and up, because a handful of expensive clicks a day is data starvation, not a strategy. Our Miami Google Ads budget guide breaks down what each level realistically buys.

What are your management fees?

A flat monthly fee scoped to the work in the account, not a percentage of spend that rewards us for spending more. Our pricing page publishes the bands we work in, so you can see whether we fit your budget before we ever get on a call.

How fast do Google Ads results come?

Faster than anything else we run. Campaigns typically produce their first leads within two to four weeks of launch, then sharpen over the first 90 days as conversion data teaches the account which searches actually book. We report against revenue from week one, and we never promise a specific number.

Should we run Local Services Ads or search ads?

Licensed trades and law firms should usually run both. Local Services Ads sit at the very top, charge per lead, and lean on your license, insurance, and reviews. Search ads give you what LSAs never will: control of keywords, copy, landing pages, and coverage for every query LSAs skip. The right mix depends on your category, your review base, and your capacity.

Who owns the ad account?

You do, always. We build and manage campaigns inside your own Google Ads account with your billing attached, and you keep full admin access the entire time. If we ever part ways, the campaigns, the data, and all the history stay with you. Agencies that hold accounts hostage are holding leverage, not standards.

Do you run Google Ads in Spanish?

Yes, as parallel builds with their own keyword research, their own ad copy, and their own landing pages, never as translated English campaigns. Roughly seven in ten Miami-Dade households speak a language other than English at home. In this county, Spanish search is not a niche. It is the market.

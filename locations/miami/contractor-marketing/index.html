The work that is out there

Miami never stops renovating. The problem is that the jobs worth having rarely go to whoever answers cheapest.

There is real money moving through this county's construction economy right now. Buildings from the boom decades are due for updates. Coral Gables and Coconut Grove owners are reworking older homes rather than leaving neighborhoods they waited years to get into. Condo units in Edgewater, Brickell, and along the beaches change hands and get gutted within a month of closing. Impact windows and doors have turned into their own market, driven by code, insurance pressure, and owners who would rather not board up in September. Florida leads the country in new business formation, and plenty of that lands in the trades, so the competition for that work arrives with a truck, a logo, and a Facebook page inside a week.

Contractors struggle online here for an unglamorous reason. The work speaks for itself in person and not at all on a phone screen. Most remodeler websites are a logo, a stock kitchen, and a contact form, so the buyer has nothing to judge except price, and price is the one comparison you lose to an unlicensed crew. Meanwhile the searches that carry actual budgets, condo renovation, impact window installation, permitted addition, get won by whoever built pages for them. Usually nobody in your radius has.

Where contractor pipelines break in this market

04 breaks

01

The marketplace tax

Lead marketplaces charge you for a homeowner they also sold to three competitors, then rank you by how fast you answer and how low you quote. You are renting access to a customer while the platform owns the relationship. Building your own pipeline costs more in month one and less every month after.

02

Estimators burning days on tire-kickers

Unqualified inquiries cost you the most expensive resource you have. A morning driving from Doral to Kendall for a homeowner with a $12,000 idea and a $60,000 plan is a morning not spent closing. Qualification belongs in the form, not in the truck.

03

Buildings have rules, and buyers can tell who knows them

In the tower neighborhoods, a renovation runs through association approval, insurance certificates, elevator reservations, work-hour windows, and a permit process that punishes guessing. A contractor who explains that on the website reads as safe. One who does not reads as a risk to the owner's closing date.

04

The photos are the pitch

Coral Gables and Coconut Grove buyers judge finish quality from a phone screen before they consider calling. Dim job-site snapshots signal dim work. Properly shot completed projects, organized by type, do more for your close rate than any headline we could write.

The funnel we install

05 stages

01

A portfolio-led landing page

Traffic lands on finished work, not on a company history. Photography first, scope and materials underneath, license and insurance in plain view. The page answers the only question a renovation buyer is really asking: can these people deliver this at this level.

02

A form that qualifies budget

Project type, property type, timeline, and a budget range presented as bands so it feels normal to answer. Fewer submissions, dramatically better ones. The people who leave were never going to sign.

03

The consultation

Booked automatically, confirmed by text, and briefed before you arrive so the conversation starts at scope instead of introductions. Qualified inquiries reach a human quickly. Everyone else gets nurtured.

04

A scoped estimate

Options, allowances, and a permit and approval timeline written out. In a market where owners have heard renovation horror stories from a neighbor, clarity closes. Vague numbers invite three more bids.

05

Patient nurture

Renovations get planned for months and paused for a closing, a board meeting, or a season. Automated follow-up keeps you present with project updates and useful answers, so when the plan restarts you are the only contractor they still remember.

The channel nobody automates

Your best Miami jobs will keep arriving through people, not platforms. Treat that like a channel and it stops being luck.

Interior designers and property managers send more high-ticket renovation work in this county than any single ad account does. A designer with clients in the Grove needs a contractor who will not embarrass them on finish detail. A property manager needs someone who follows building rules and does not create complaints on floor eleven. Both are repeat sources, and both are usually courted by nobody but the last contractor who did them a favor.

So we build for it. A trade-facing page that shows how you work with designers, a capabilities piece property managers can forward to a board, a simple sequence that keeps you in front of past referrers between projects, and a website presentable enough that a designer is comfortable sending a client to it. That is the difference between marketing and revenue infrastructure: the system feeds every path to a signed contract, not just the paid ones. Miami contractor questions, answered

How do we compete with cheap unlicensed operators?

You stop competing on price and start competing on certainty. An unlicensed crew wins the homeowner who compares nothing but numbers, and that homeowner was never your customer. We put license, insurance, permit fluency, and finished work where nobody has to look for them, and we target searches that carry intent to hire properly. In a county where an unpermitted remodel can fail inspection or stall a closing, doing it correctly is a selling point rather than an overhead line.

What lead volume is realistic for high-ticket work?

Less than a lead app would promise you, and worth far more. A full condo renovation is a decision measured in months and in tens of thousands of dollars, so a healthy month looks like a handful of genuinely qualified consultations, not fifty form fills. We tune the system toward booked consultations and signed scopes. Volume that fills a calendar with people who wanted a ballpark is not progress.

Do you help qualify leads?

Yes, and it is most of the value. Budget range, project type, property type, timeline, and whether the unit sits in a building with association approvals all get asked before anything reaches your calendar. Inquiries that do not qualify enter a nurture sequence instead of an appointment slot. Your estimators should be driving across the county for scoped work only.

What does contractor marketing cost in Miami?

Most Miami contractors and remodelers we work with typically invest between $3,000 and $10,000 per month across search, paid campaigns, and the website itself. One signed renovation often covers a full quarter of that, which is why high-ticket trades can afford a real system when thin-margin businesses cannot. We publish the bands we work in and we break the numbers down further on our blog.

How long until jobs come in?

Paid search paired with a real landing page can produce consultations in the first two to four weeks. Signed contracts trail behind, because renovation buyers plan for months, collect approvals, and consult a designer before committing. SEO in this market usually shows meaningful movement in three to six months. We never promise rankings. We build the system that earns them and report on booked consultations and contract value the whole way.

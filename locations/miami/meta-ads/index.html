The market

Miami is one of the most Instagram-native markets in America. This city discovers visually, then buys from the feed.

Wynwood built an entire economy on being photographed. Restaurants here get chosen from a plate on a screen long before anyone reads a menu, which is why our Miami restaurant marketing playbook leans so hard on the feed. Preconstruction towers in Edgewater sell from renders and drone footage, and half of Miami real estate marketing is a scroll-stopping walkthrough. Med spas run on before-and-afters, gyms on transformations, events on last year's aftermovie. In this market, the feed is the storefront.

Owners feel this instinctively, which is why most have already tried. They posted, they boosted, a few hundred likes arrived, and nothing booked. The conclusion they reach is that Instagram does not work for their business. The real problem is that a boosted post is not a campaign, and Meta will happily let you spend like it is.

The boost trap

The Boost button is Meta's easiest sale and Miami's most common waste. Boosting buys applause. Campaigns buy customers.

Boosting optimizes for engagement, so Meta dutifully finds people who like things, not people who buy things. There is no pixel strategy behind it, no conversion event, no lead form, no exclusion of existing customers, no retargeting pool being built for later. It is reach with nothing underneath, which is why the likes pile up while the calendar stays empty.

A real build runs in Ads Manager with the pixel and Conversions API wired to outcomes that matter: lead submitted, appointment booked, order placed. Leads land in the CRM, revenue flows back against the campaign, and every report leads with cost per booked customer instead of engagement rate. That is the standard because Skybound does not sell posts or impressions; we build complete customer acquisition systems, and paid social is one engine inside the machine. Our pricing page publishes the bands we work in, and our guide to getting more leads in Miami shows where Meta fits in the wider mix.

Miami Meta ads questions, answered

Do Facebook ads still work in Miami?

Better here than almost anywhere, when they are run properly. Miami discovers restaurants, real estate, aesthetics, and events visually, and Facebook and Instagram are where that discovery happens. What stopped working is the lazy version: boosted posts, stock imagery, English-only creative in a bilingual county. Campaigns built on strong creative and real audience strategy are a different sport.

What budget do Meta ads need in Miami?

Most local campaigns start well at $1,500 to $3,000 per month in ad spend, enough to test several creative angles and build a retargeting pool. Ecommerce and multi-location businesses scale up from there. The first month buys data more than customers; the winners it reveals are what the following months scale.

Do you produce the ad creative?

Yes, creative is most of the job. We write the hooks, script and edit the reels, design the statics, and develop the Spanish concepts. From you we need the raw truth of the business: a shoot at your location, project photos, phone footage of real work, and ideally a founder willing to spend thirty minutes on camera. Real beats stock in this market, every time.

Why not just boost posts?

Because boosting optimizes for engagement, so Meta finds people who like things, not people who buy things. There is no conversion event, no lead form, no retargeting pool, no exclusion of existing customers. Boosting is fine to give an announcement extra reach. As an acquisition channel, it is a donation to Meta.

How do you measure revenue from Meta ads?

With the pixel and Conversions API wired to outcomes that matter: leads into the CRM, purchases into the ad account, booked appointments tagged back to the campaign that produced them. Every report connects spend to leads and leads to revenue. A campaign that wins applause but books nothing gets cut, whatever its engagement rate says.

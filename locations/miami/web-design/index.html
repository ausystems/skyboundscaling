The standard

Miami is a design-literate city. Your customers calibrate against luxury every day, and your website gets graded on that curve.

Think about what the people you want as customers walk past before lunch. Flagship retail in the Design District. Hotel lobbies on Collins Avenue that cost more than most commercial builds. Sales galleries in Edgewater selling condos off renderings alone. Restaurant brands that spend more on a menu redesign than many companies spend on their entire web presence. That is the visual environment your website competes inside, whether you build homes, run a clinic, or manage a law practice.

The penalty for looking dated is quiet and expensive. Nobody emails to say your site feels old. A homeowner in Coral Gables just closes the tab and calls the builder whose portfolio looks like the seven-figure renovation she wants. A patient comparing med spas on Miami Beach books the one whose site feels as clean as the treatment room. Fair or not, in this city a dated website reads as a dated business, and a premium one reads as proof that you sweat the details.

What a Miami website has to get right

04 standards

01

Fast on a phone, first

Most of your visitors arrive on a phone, often on a cell connection in a car or on a job site. We build to a strict performance budget: light pages, compressed images, no bloated page builders. Speed is a ranking factor and a patience factor, and Miami has none of the latter.

02

Bilingual by design, not by widget

Roughly seven in ten Miami-Dade households speak a language other than English at home. If Spanish matters to your customers, we architect it in from the start: a real Spanish version with its own URLs, human copy, and forms, not an auto-translate button bolted on after launch.

03

Trust a stranger can verify

Miami buyers read reviews before they believe a word you say about yourself. So we surface license numbers for the trades, insurance and affiliations, Google reviews pulled onto the page, and real project photography instead of stock. One genuine photo of your crew on a Kendall roof outsells ten stock renderings of glass towers.

04

One obvious next step

Above the fold, a first-time visitor should know what you do, where you do it, and what to tap. Short forms, tap-to-call, booking built in. After launch we pressure-test all of it with Miami conversion optimization so the design keeps earning its keep.

Site architecture

A Miami service business does not need a big website. It needs a small set of pages, each with one job.

The pattern that works here is consistent: a home page that positions you in five seconds, one dedicated page per service instead of a single crowded list, area pages for the parts of Miami-Dade you actually serve, a portfolio with real projects, a reviews page that concentrates your proof, an about page with faces and credentials, and a contact page where booking takes one step, not three. Service and area pages do double duty, because they are the pages that can earn rankings for the searches that pay. That is exactly where our Miami SEO work plugs in.

Just as important is what we leave out: sliders nobody swipes, a blog with three abandoned posts, walls of badges no customer reads. Every page has to earn its place. We keep a running record of the failures we see most across local sites, from hidden phone numbers to English-only booking flows, in our breakdown of Miami website design mistakes.

Web design questions, answered

How much does a website cost in Miami?

Focused projects like a single landing page with automation typically come in under $10,000. Most full marketing sites we build for established Miami businesses land in the $10,000 to $25,000 band, with larger bilingual or content-heavy builds above that. Our pricing page publishes the bands, so you can scope honestly before we ever talk.

How long does a Miami website build take?

A focused landing page ships in two to three weeks. A full marketing site typically runs six to ten weeks from strategy to launch, with the schedule set in week one. The variable is rarely design; it is how fast photography, content, and approvals move on your side.

Do you redesign existing websites?

Yes, and most of our Miami work is exactly that: a site that looks fine but does not produce customers. We audit what you have, keep what earns rankings and leads, rebuild what does not, and map every redirect so the search equity you have built survives the switch.

Can the site be in English and Spanish?

Yes, and in Miami-Dade it usually should be. We design bilingual navigation from the start, write the Spanish properly instead of machine-translating it, and set the technical signals so Google serves each visitor the right version. Follow-up then continues in the language the lead chose.

Will the new site rank on Google?

A well-built site is the foundation: fast, structured, and indexable from day one. Rankings are then earned through ongoing work on content, links, and reviews, and nobody can honestly promise positions. That ongoing work is what our Miami SEO service exists to do.

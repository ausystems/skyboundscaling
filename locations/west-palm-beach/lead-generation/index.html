The broker economy

Palm Beach County work is valuable enough that an entire industry exists to sell you the same customer four times. Buy leads and you are paying to enter a race you did not choose.

The economics here make it worse than average. An estate-scale renovation, a trust consultation downtown, a listing on the water, a concierge medicine membership: these are worth multiples of a typical Florida inquiry, so the broker platforms price accordingly and sell each one more than once to cover their own acquisition cost. The homeowner filled out a single form on a single website. Twenty minutes later four companies are calling, and from that moment the work is not won on craft, credentials, or the twelve years you spent building a reputation in this county. It is won by whoever dialed first and whoever blinked on price. Then the broker sells you that same household again next quarter.

A funnel you own ends the race before it starts. The prospect finds your offer, on your page, and reaches your team alone, because nobody else got a notification. You are not discounting against three companies you have never met. You are not renting a pipeline that switches off the day you stop paying. And every name, number, and note about what that person wanted stays in your database permanently, which over a few seasons is worth more than any single month of purchased leads. That is the difference between buying a deliverable and owning a customer acquisition system.

The county lead system, step by step

06 steps

01

An offer engineered for who actually buys here

Free estimate is not an offer to a buyer who is not price shopping first. This clientele is risk shopping: who is competent, who is discreet, who will not waste their time. So the offer gets a name and a stated value. A private design consultation. A second opinion. A property review before the season. A confidential case assessment. Everything downstream inherits the quality of that first promise, which is why we write it before anyone opens a design file. The wider method lives on our lead generation page.

02

A landing page that reads established

The person evaluating you may have spent that morning across the Intracoastal, where nothing is unfinished. A page that loads slowly, leans on stock photography of a kitchen that is clearly not in Florida, or buries the phone number reads as risk before a word is processed. Ours load fast, show real work, name the cities served, put proof above the fold, and ask for exactly one thing.

03

Traffic from search and social, for different reasons

Search captures the person who has already decided they need you and is choosing between names. Social reaches the person who has not thought about it yet, which is most of the county in July. We run both and feed one funnel: West Palm Beach Google Ads for existing intent, Meta ads for demand we create, each measured on leads rather than impressions.

04

Qualification that respects budget bands

The most expensive hour in a county service business is the one an estimator spends driving from Jupiter to Boca Raton for a job that was never funded. Two hours of the day, the fuel, and a slot a real customer wanted. So the form and the first follow-up ask the uncomfortable questions early: scope, timeline, budget range, who else decides, whether the property sits in a community with approval requirements. Inquiries that do not clear the band go to nurture instead of into a truck.

05

Instant follow-up, because minutes decide it

A lead answered in under five minutes converts far better than the same lead answered an hour later, and this buyer in particular moves straight to the next name without a second thought. So the response fires in seconds: a text, an email, a task for whoever is on call, a booking link that fills the calendar. It runs at 9pm on a Sunday and through the whole of August. That spine is our AI automation work, wired in from day one rather than added later.

06

Patient nurture, because it is never one person deciding

High-value decisions here rarely close on the first call. There is a spouse. Often a financial advisor or an attorney who reviews anything above a threshold. Sometimes a property manager who runs the house while the owners are north, and sometimes an architectural committee that meets once a month and has opinions about roof tile. So the sequence is built in weeks, not hours: genuinely useful emails, a check-in that does not beg, seasonal reasons to re-engage, and a pipeline stage that stops a slow yes from being filed as a no.

What counts as a lead

The definition

  • Booked consults
  • Showings
  • Qualified estimates
  • Case evaluations
  • Event inquiries
  • Demo calls

Never a form fill. For a practice, a lead is an appointment on the schedule with insurance verified. For a brokerage it is a showing or a listing appointment on the calendar, which is the only number our real estate marketing reports. For a contractor it is an estimate with scope and budget range confirmed before anyone drives across the county. For a firm it is a case evaluation that cleared conflict and merit screening. For a venue or a caterer it is an inquiry with a date, a headcount, and a number attached. We agree on the definition before launch, and then that is the only figure we report against.

Season as pipeline

Every business in this county fights over November through April. The ones that win it were quietly building the list in July.

Summer is not dead time, it is list-building time. Costs per click and per lead fall while half the county is up north, which makes June through September the cheapest window of the year to collect contacts. So we run the top of the funnel hard while it is quiet: guides, checklists, waitlists, early-booking incentives for work that has to be finished before the owners fly back, and offers aimed at the year-round residents who are easiest to reach when nobody else is bidding. Then we nurture that list through the fall, so by the time flights start landing the pipeline is already full of people who know your name.

Harvest season is a different job. From November the emphasis moves to speed and capacity: bookings, calendars, response times, and enough people to answer the phone at 4:55 on a Friday. Trades run their own version of this rhythm around storm season, where one forecast pulls a month of demand into a single week and the company with a warm list of past inquiries fills its schedule before a competitor has finished writing an ad. Same principle either way. The pipeline gets built when it is cheap and spent when it is valuable.

West Palm Beach lead generation, answered

Are the leads exclusive?

Always. We do not resell, we do not broker, and nothing the funnel produces goes to another business. It runs on your offer, your landing page, and your ad account, so the inquiry reaches you and nobody else. That is the whole difference between a system you own and a subscription to somebody else's list.

What does a lead cost in Palm Beach County?

It depends on how competitive your category is and how much a client is worth to you. Home services and consumer categories typically land in the tens of dollars per lead. Estate legal, concierge medical, and high-ticket renovation run into the hundreds, because the clicks cost more and one signed client can cover a season of spend. We model a range from your numbers before launch, and we will not promise a cost per lead we cannot see yet.

How fast do leads start coming in?

The build takes two to four weeks: the offer, the page, the tracking, and the follow-up automation. Paid traffic usually produces the first leads within days of going live, then the cost settles as campaigns learn. The nurture side takes longer to show its value, because the contacts collected in month one are often the deals that close in month four.

Which industries fit this best?

Anything with a client value high enough to justify a real system: renovation and home services, medical and dental practices, real estate, law firms, financial and wealth services, marine, event venues, and B2B companies selling into county businesses. If your average client is worth a few hundred dollars and you need pure volume, paid media and SEO usually serve you better than a funnel build.

Do you also close the leads?

No. Closing belongs to your team and they will always do it better than an outside agency. What we do is make sure nothing goes cold on the way there: a response in seconds, reminders, rescheduling, and a nurture sequence that keeps a slow prospect warm for weeks, so your team is calling people who still remember why they raised a hand.

The market

Palm Beach County holds one of the oldest and wealthiest patient bases in the country. Every November, thousands of those patients need new doctors in the same month.

The infrastructure keeps growing to meet them. Hospital campuses keep expanding, medical office corridors keep filling in around them, and specialists keep opening second locations between downtown West Palm Beach and the northern suburbs. Healthcare marketing in Palm Beach County is not about convincing anyone to seek care. The demand is structural. The work is being the practice a patient finds, trusts, and books when the need arrives.

Then the season sharpens everything. Seasonal residents return from November through April, and the new arrivals among them need a primary care physician, a dentist, a dermatologist, and often a cardiologist or an orthopedist within weeks of landing. They have no local referral network, so they choose the way modern patients choose: they search, they read reviews closely, and they book whoever makes it easy. Meanwhile concierge and cash-pay medicine is growing alongside Medicare-heavy panels, which means positioning is no longer optional. A practice has to say plainly who it serves and how, or the market decides for it.

The November patient has no one to ask

A retiree who just arrived from New Jersey needs a physician this month and knows nobody local to ask. Google is the referral. Our Palm Beach County local SEO guide breaks down how those searches get decided, and practices missing from the map pack are simply not in the running.

02

Reviews decide, and most physicians leave them to chance

This patient base reads reviews the way it once read references: carefully, and with alternatives open in the next tab. An excellent physician with a dozen aging reviews loses to a good one with hundreds of recent ones. In new patient marketing here, reputation is not a nice-to-have. It is the deciding factor.

03

The phone is still the front door

An older patient base books by phone first, and seasonal call volume swamps front desks exactly when new patients are calling. Every hold that ends in a hangup, and every voicemail after five, is a patient dialing the next practice on the list.

04

One website trying to serve two economies

A concierge internist and a Medicare-heavy group sell different things: access and time on one side, coverage and convenience on the other. A website that blurs the two converts neither. Positioning gets decided before a dollar goes to ads.

Paid social, handled with care

Medical advertising has rules for good reason. Careful and effective are not opposites. They are the job.

Meta ads earn their place in this market when they are used precisely. Facebook and Instagram reach the county's year-round residents and its seasonal ones, introduce a physician before the need turns urgent, and retarget the people who read a service page and paused. For med spas, dermatology, and concierge medicine, paid social is often the strongest channel of all, because those decisions begin with curiosity rather than symptoms.

The care is in the construction. No protected health information touches targeting or creative; audiences are built from geography, season, and intent, never from patient data. Testimonials run only with written consent. Claims stay factual and verifiable. Specialists get one more layer of judgment: campaigns tuned to balance physician referral relationships with direct-to-patient visibility, so the marketing supports the referral network instead of stepping on it.

Palm Beach County medical marketing, answered

Is medical advertising HIPAA-sensitive?

Yes, and we build for it from the first day. No protected health information enters ad targeting, audiences, or creative; we target geography, season, and intent instead of patient data. Testimonials and patient photos run only with written consent, and every claim stays factual and verifiable. The campaigns stay effective because the strategy never depended on patient data in the first place.

How do you capture the November patient influx?

By being established before it starts. We build condition pages, service pages, and review volume through the summer so rankings and reputation are in place when seasonal residents start searching. In October, ad budgets rise, and they hold through April while new arrivals rebuild their entire care roster from scratch. A practice that starts marketing in December pays peak prices for what is left. The calendar is the strategy.

What does a new patient cost in Palm Beach County?

It depends on the specialty and the payer mix. A Medicare wellness visit and a concierge membership are different economics, so we track cost per booked appointment by service line and judge it against what a retained patient is worth over years, not against the first visit. We scope budgets from your capacity backward, and our pricing page publishes the bands we work in.

What is the reviews strategy for physicians?

Volume, recency, and response. We automate one well-timed review request after visits, which steadily builds recent reviews on Google and the health directories patients actually read. Every review gets a response that thanks the reviewer without confirming anyone was a patient, which protects privacy and looks attentive to the next reader. For a new arrival with no local referrals, your review page is the referral.

Do you work with concierge practices?

Yes. Concierge and cash-pay medicine is growing quickly in Palm Beach County, and it needs different marketing than an insurance-driven practice: positioning that justifies the membership, content that sells access and time rather than procedures, and follow-up polished enough for a patient who expects white-glove everything. We build to that standard.

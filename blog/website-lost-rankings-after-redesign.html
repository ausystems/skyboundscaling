Why your website lost rankings after a redesign.

A sharp drop after launch is almost always one of five fixable causes. How to diagnose each in minutes and the recovery sequence that brings positions back.

Published August 3, 2026By Skybound ScalingToronto, ON

First: confirm what actually dropped

Before touching anything, open Search Console and separate three different events that all feel like the sky falling: a drop in impressions, a drop in average position, and a drop in clicks. Then check whether the drop lines up with your redesign launch date, a Google core update, or a season your business always dips. Redesign damage shows a sharp cliff within days of launch and it clusters on specific pages. If that is your shape, one of the five causes below is almost certainly responsible.

Cause 1: URLs changed without redirects

The most common killer. The new site gave pages new addresses, the old addresses now 404, and every ranking and backlink attached to them is orphaned. Diagnose in minutes: crawl your old URL list and count the 404s. Fix by shipping 301 redirects from every old URL to its closest new equivalent. Redirect to relevant pages, not the homepage, and avoid chains. Recovery usually follows within weeks of the fix. This is why the redirect map is the centerpiece of our redesign checklist.

Cause 2: The content got thinner

Redesigns love minimalism, and minimalism loves deleting paragraphs. The paragraphs were what ranked. Compare old and new versions of your biggest traffic pages: if a page went from twelve hundred words of substance to two hundred words of vibes, Google noticed. Restore the depth in the new design language: collapsible sections, richer service descriptions, FAQs. Depth and elegance are not enemies, and the FAQ pattern in particular preserves both.

Cause 3: Technical regressions

  • Accidental noindex. Staging settings that shipped to production. Check the page source and Search Console indexing report first: it is a one line fix that resurrects everything.
  • Broken canonicals. Pages pointing canonical tags at the wrong URL, or every page pointing at the homepage.
  • Slower pages. The new design shipped with heavier assets and red Core Web Vitals. Speed regressions cost positions in competitive results.
  • Lost structured data. The old site had schema markup, the new one silently dropped it, and rich results vanished with it.

Cause 4: Heading and internal link structure collapsed

New templates often flatten the document outline: multiple h1s, decorative headings out of order, and the quiet death of internal links that used to route authority to money pages. Crawl the new site and compare internal link counts to important pages against the old site. Restore descriptive anchor links from related content, and give every page exactly one h1 with a sane outline underneath.

The recovery sequence

Week 1: ship redirects, remove any stray noindex, fix canonicals. These reverse the sharpest losses. Weeks 2 to 3: restore content depth on the ten most valuable pages and repair internal linking. Week 4 onward: resubmit the sitemap, request recrawls of key pages, and watch Search Console coverage while positions rebuild. Most redesign drops recover substantially within four to eight weeks of proper fixes. The rankings were never destroyed, just disconnected. And if the drop does not match any cause here, compare dates against known Google updates before assuming the redesign did it: correlation is not the same as a missing redirect.

Questions, answered

How long does it take to recover rankings after a redesign?

With the real cause fixed, most sites recover substantially within four to eight weeks. Redirect fixes show the fastest turnarounds, sometimes within days, because the authority was disconnected rather than lost.

Should I roll back my new website?

Rarely necessary. The new design is usually fine; the migration hygiene was not. Fixing redirects, content depth, and technical regressions on the new site beats retreating to the old one.

Can a Google update look like redesign damage?

Yes. If your drop coincides with a documented core update rather than your launch date, the diagnosis and the fix are different. Always compare both dates before acting.

Do I need an SEO during a redesign, not just after?

It is dramatically cheaper that way. An SEO involved before launch maps redirects and protects structure. Recovery work after the fact costs more and hurts for weeks. Our process builds this in.

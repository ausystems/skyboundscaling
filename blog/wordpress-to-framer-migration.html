WordPress to Framer, without losing rankings.

The five step migration we run for clients: inventory, rebuild, content, the redirect map that protects your authority, and the launch checks that catch problems early.

Why businesses are leaving WordPress

WordPress still powers a huge share of the web, and for some sites it remains the right tool. But the complaints that bring businesses to us repeat almost word for word: plugin stacks that break on update, hosting that needs babysitting, page builders stacked on page builders, slow mobile scores nobody can fix, and a site the marketing team is afraid to touch. Framer solves that specific cluster of problems: no plugins to maintain, hosting included, fast by default, and editing a marketer can do safely. The catch is getting there without dropping the rankings your WordPress site spent years earning. That is what this guide is for.

Step 1: Inventory everything

Crawl your WordPress site and export every URL, then pull Search Console and analytics data for the last twelve months. Sort pages into three buckets: pages that earn traffic or links and must move carefully, pages you still need but that earn nothing, and dead weight to retire. Most WordPress sites are shocked by bucket three: years of tag archives, thin posts, and auto generated pages that dilute the site. A migration is the cheapest moment you will ever have to fix that.

Step 2: Rebuild structure and design in Framer

Recreate your URL structure in Framer where it is sound, and improve it where it is not. Keep slugs identical for your load bearing pages when possible: the fewer URLs that change, the less redirect risk you carry. This is also the moment to rebuild the design around conversion rather than around what a theme allowed: one job per page, proof where buyers need it, and the speed Framer gives you by default.

Step 3: Move content deliberately

Blog posts and case studies move into the Framer CMS. Resist the temptation to bulk paste: the migration is your chance to update stale posts, merge overlapping ones, and rewrite titles and descriptions that never earned their click. Carry over your metadata deliberately, page by page for the pages that matter. Content that moves thoughtfully tends to rank better after migration, not just equal.

Step 4: The redirect map, the whole game

Every WordPress URL that changes or dies gets a 301 redirect to its closest living equivalent, set up inside Framer before launch. Three rules keep this safe: redirect to the most relevant page rather than dumping everything on the homepage, avoid chains where A points to B points to C, and keep the map in a spreadsheet you can audit. For a typical business site this is an afternoon of careful work that protects years of accumulated authority.

Step 5: Launch and verify

  • Point the domain to Framer and confirm SSL is active
  • Submit the new sitemap in Search Console the same day
  • Spot check your top pages: metadata intact, headings sane, analytics firing
  • Crawl the live site for broken links and redirect errors
  • Watch Search Console coverage and positions daily for two weeks

Expect small ranking wobbles for a week or two as Google recrawls. Sustained drops mean a missed redirect or a thinned page, both findable in the crawl. Our post on why sites lose rankings after a redesign is the debugging companion to this guide.

What changes after the move

The practical wins arrive immediately: no plugin updates, no hosting maintenance, mobile scores that pass without tuning, and edits your team ships in minutes. The strategic win compounds: because publishing is easy, publishing happens, and the content engine that SEO depends on actually runs. That is the honest case for the move, and our platform comparison covers when it is not the right call.

Questions, answered

Will I lose my Google rankings moving from WordPress to Framer?

Not if the migration is done properly. Rankings drop from missed redirects, thinned content, or performance regressions, not from the platform change itself. The redirect map is the critical artifact.

Can I move my WordPress blog into Framer?

Yes. Posts move into the Framer CMS, ideally with a cleanup pass that updates stale content and merges overlap. Slugs and metadata should be carried deliberately for every post that earns traffic.

How long does a WordPress to Framer migration take?

A typical marketing site migrates in two to five weeks including redesign. A like for like content move with light design updates can go faster.

What happens to my WordPress plugins?

Most exist to patch things Framer does natively: SEO controls, forms, speed, backups. Genuine functionality like gated portals or complex ecommerce needs a purpose built solution and gets scoped before the move.

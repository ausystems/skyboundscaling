Website design mistakes Miami businesses keep making.

Eight problems we find in almost every Miami site audit. The symptom you can check yourself, why each one costs more here than it would anywhere else, and the fix.

How Miami sites lose quietly

Most websites here do not fail loudly. They convert at a fraction of what they should, month after month, until the owner concludes that marketing does not work in Miami.

We audit a lot of these. The same eight problems surface again and again, across Brickell law firms, Coral Gables practices, Doral logistics companies, and trades working the whole county. None are exotic and all are fixable. Where a legal or home service click is among the most expensive in the country, a site that leaks half its traffic is a budget problem wearing a design costume.

Each mistake below has the symptom you can check this afternoon, the reason it costs more in Miami, and the fix. If you would rather have the whole thing rebuilt around conversion, that is what our Miami web design work is for. Start by finding out which of these you have.

1. It loads slowly on mobile, in a city that searches on the move

The symptom. Open your own site on cellular data, not office wifi, while sitting in a car. If the hero image has not painted by three seconds, you have this problem. The usual culprits are uncompressed photos, tracking scripts nobody has audited in two years, and a page builder loading every module it knows.

Why it costs money here. Miami searches in traffic. People look you up from the Dolphin, from a driveway in Kendall, from a Brickell parking garage where the signal drops to one bar. That person does not wait. They hit back and click the next result, and Google reads that as a bad answer. Every slow second is paid for twice: once in the visitor, once in the ranking signal you hand a competitor.

The fix. Compress and size every image, serve modern formats, remove unused scripts, and set a page weight budget your team has to respect. Then test on a real phone on cellular, not only in a lab tool on a fast connection.

2. The phone number is hiding behind a menu

The symptom. Open the homepage on a phone. Can a visitor call you in one tap without opening a menu or scrolling? If not, you have this problem. The classic version buries Contact three items down a hamburger menu, with a form at the bottom of a long page.

Why it costs money here. A large share of Miami service inquiries are phone calls, and many are urgent: a leak through a ceiling, an air conditioning failure in August, an arrest, a table for eight tonight. They are not filling out a form and waiting. They want a tap-to-call button, an address they can push into Waze, and hours they can trust. Making them hunt sends them to whoever made it easy.

The fix. Tap-to-call in the header on every page, a sticky call or book bar on mobile, and the number in real text so it can be copied, dialed, and read by search engines. Clinics and restaurants should embed booking rather than linking to a link. If your team takes calls in Spanish, say so.

3. It only speaks English, in a bilingual market

The symptom. No Spanish version, no language toggle, no Spanish content of any kind, while a significant share of your customers speak Spanish at home.

Why it costs money here. Miami-Dade is a majority bilingual market. Roughly seven in ten households speak a language other than English at home, Spanish first. Those customers search in Spanish, read reviews in Spanish, and hire the business that makes it obvious they will be served in Spanish. An English-only site is not neutral here. It signals that you are not for a large part of the market, and it leaves a layer of much less contested queries untouched.

The fix. Start with the pages that make money: main service pages, contact page, top landing pages. Have them written by someone fluent in the Spanish people actually speak here, because a machine-translated medical or legal page reads as careless to a native speaker. Give each version its own indexable URL. A translate widget in the corner neither ranks nor convinces anyone.

4. Stock photography, in a market that judges finish quality

The symptom. A handshake in a boardroom. A model in scrubs. A skyline that is not even Miami. Nothing was photographed at your business, by anyone who works there.

Why it costs money here. Miami buyers, especially at the premium end, are unusually good at spotting the generic. A homeowner in Coral Gables comparing three renovation firms, or a buyer on the Beach choosing a med spa, treats your imagery as evidence of the work itself. Stock photography answers the question they were already asking: either you have nothing worth showing, or you are newer than you imply. The city trains this instinct: people move through Wynwood, the Design District, and Brickell all week, surrounded by businesses that spent real money on presentation.

The fix. One professional shoot: your space, your team, your finished work. It is among the highest-return line items here and it dates slowly. If a shoot is not in budget this quarter, well-composed phone photos of genuine work still beat polished stock every time.

5. No proof above the fold

The symptom. Load the homepage on a phone and stop before the first scroll. Is there a rating, a review count, a client logo, a license number, or a recognizable project? If the first screen is a slogan over a photo, you have this problem.

Why it costs money here. Florida leads the country in new business formation and a large share lands in Miami-Dade, so buyers here are surrounded by companies that did not exist last year. They have adapted by verifying almost everything before they call. If your first screen does not show that other people already trusted you, visitors leave to check you on Google, where your competitors sit one result away.

The fix. Put the strongest proof you have into the first screen: star rating and review count, years in business, license, the buildings or neighborhoods you have worked in. Then repeat proof beside every call to action further down, because that is where hesitation shows up.

6. Template sameness in premium verticals

The symptom. Open your site and two competitors' sites in three tabs. Same hero layout, same row of three icon cards, same stock team section. If you could swap the logos and nobody would notice, you have this problem.

Why it costs money here. In some categories sameness is survivable. In Miami's premium verticals it is not. Aesthetics-driven practices, luxury real estate, high-end hospitality, and boutique legal firms in the Gables and Brickell all sell judgment and taste. When three of them present identically, the buyer has nothing left to choose on except price, and you have commoditized yourself with your own template.

The fix. You do not need an experimental website. You need a distinct one: real photography, a typographic voice that belongs to you, a layout built around your actual offer, and copy a competitor could not paste onto their page. A returning visitor should recognize you before reading a word.

7. Nothing is tracked, so every decision runs on feelings

The symptom. You cannot answer, in under a minute, how many leads the site produced last month and where they came from. Or analytics is installed but with no conversion events, no call tracking, and no form goals, which amounts to the same thing.

Why it costs money here. Miami click prices in legal and home services are among the highest in the country, so paid traffic into an untracked site is the most expensive way to learn nothing. You cannot tell which campaign produced the job that closed, so you cut the channel that was quietly working and double the one that was not. Owners here almost always conclude that online marketing does not work, when nobody wired up the scoreboard.

The fix. Analytics with real conversion events, call tracking on the phone number, and form submissions tied to source, so you can connect a booked job back to a click. Then actually look at it every month. Our conversion rate optimization in Miami work starts here, because you cannot improve a page you are not measuring.

8. It is a brochure, not the top of a funnel

The symptom. The site describes the business. About page, services page, contact form, and nothing that gives a visitor a reason to act today. The only call to action is "contact us."

Why it costs money here. Miami buyers compare fast, usually on a phone, usually across three tabs at once. If your site offers no next step smaller than a phone call and no reason to move now, you lose everyone who was interested but not yet ready, which is most visitors. In a market where you paid real money for that visit, letting them leave with nothing is the most expensive habit here.

The fix. Give the visit a job. One clear primary action per page. A genuine top-of-funnel offer: a free inspection, a same-week consult, a pricing guide. A second, lower-commitment option for people not ready to call. Then retargeting, so the ones who leave still see you again. And if the traffic is not arriving in the first place, why your Miami business is not ranking on Google covers the visibility half of the same problem.

The order to fix them in

You do not need a rebuild to start. Work in this order, because each step raises the value of the next.

  1. Tracking. You cannot judge anything else without it, and it takes hours rather than weeks.
  2. Contact access. Tap-to-call and a sticky mobile bar are a one-day change that usually moves the number immediately.
  3. Speed. Images and scripts first, before anyone touches the design.
  4. Proof. Pull your rating, reviews, and credentials above the fold.
  5. Offer. Give people a reason to act today instead of a form that waits for them.
  6. Photography and Spanish content. Both take real effort and both pay for years.
  7. The redesign, if the structure cannot carry the six items above.

Most sites we look at need steps one through five, and those five together often do more for lead volume than a full rebuild would have. That is the honest version, and usually the cheaper one. A rebuild earns its cost when the site is slow at the foundation, when the brand has outgrown the design, or when the structure cannot support the pages you need to rank.

Either way, the website is not a deliverable you buy once. It is the front end of a revenue system, and it should be measured on booked jobs rather than compliments.

Questions, answered

How do I know if my website is costing me leads?

Compare traffic to inquiries. If people land on the site every month and almost nobody calls or submits a form, the problem is on the page rather than in the marketing. Then open your site on a phone using cellular data and try to contact yourself in one tap.

Should I redesign or optimize what I already have?

Optimize first in most cases. Tracking, contact access, speed, visible proof, and a clear offer can usually be added to an existing site, and together they often move lead volume more than a rebuild would. Rebuild when the site is slow at the foundation or cannot support the pages you need.

What does a proper business website cost in Miami?

It varies widely by scope, so treat any single number with suspicion. A credible local business site with real photography, proper tracking, and conversion-focused pages typically lands from the mid four figures into the low five figures. Bilingual, multi-location, and booking-heavy builds run higher.

How important is Spanish content for a Miami site?

For most consumer-facing and local service businesses in Miami-Dade it is important. Roughly seven in ten households here speak a language other than English at home, Spanish first, and those customers search and buy in Spanish. Start with the pages that make money and use a fluent writer rather than a translation plugin.

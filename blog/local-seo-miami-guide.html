Local SEO in Miami: the complete guide.

How local rankings actually work

Google ranks local results on three inputs it names openly: relevance, distance, and prominence. Every tactic below moves one of the three. Nothing else you do to a website changes a map pack position, which is why general SEO advice quietly fails so many Miami service businesses.

Relevance: does Google understand what you sell?

Relevance is the match between what somebody typed and what your profile and site say you do. It comes from your Business Profile categories, your services list, and the depth of your pages. A Coral Gables practice listed only as "dentist" is relevant to one search. The practice a block away, listed as a cosmetic dentist with secondaries for implants and emergency care, is relevant to dozens.

Distance: measured from the searcher, not from downtown

Distance is how far you sit from the phone doing the searching. Miami-Dade runs from Aventura down past Homestead, and the same three words typed in Kendall and in Wynwood return different businesses. You cannot argue with geography. You can only earn enough relevance and prominence that Google widens the radius it will consider you for.

Prominence: how well known you are

Prominence covers reviews, local links, press mentions, and how many people search your brand name. It is the slowest input to move. It is why a Little Havana restaurant with a decade of reviews holds off a better-looking competitor that opened in the spring.

The sections below run in the order we work them inside a Miami SEO engagement: profile, reviews, citations, website, schema, Spanish, then measurement. The early items move in weeks and buy patience for the slow ones.

Google Business Profile, done properly

For most Miami service businesses the profile earns more in year one than the website does. It is free, and on a phone it fills the screen. Most profiles we audit are two thirds complete, and the missing third is where the rankings live.

Categories decide more than anything else

Your primary category is the strongest single relevance signal in local search. Pick the one matching the service you most want to sell, not the broadest label that technically covers everything. A firm doing kitchens, baths, and additions should decide whether "general contractor" or "kitchen remodeler" is the better primary, because that choice decides which searches it can compete in at all. Add secondaries for every line you genuinely run, and none for work you do not perform.

The services list is a field almost nobody fills

Underneath your categories, Google lets you list individual services and describe each one. On most Miami profiles it sits empty. Fill it completely, name services the way customers say them rather than the way your trade does, and write two or three real sentences under each. In crowded categories this is one of the few relevance levers still going unused.

Photos: yours, not the stock library

Stock photography is recognizable at a glance and it costs you calls. Upload real work: the crew on a job, the actual storefront, your team, before and after sets. Geotagging images does nothing, whatever you may have read. Volume and freshness do. A profile adding images monthly reads as an operating business; one showing six photos from 2022 reads as closed.

The details most businesses skip

Posts, questions, attributes, and booking links separate a complete profile from a competitive one. Publish something weekly: a finished job, a reminder before hurricane season, a seasonal offer. Seed your own questions and answers with what your front desk explains ten times a day, because an empty section eventually gets answered by strangers.

The review system

Reviews are the loudest prominence signal you actually control, and here they do double duty. Miami turns over constantly, so a large share of the people searching for you arrived recently and have no neighbor to ask. Your review profile becomes the referral they never got.

Build a rhythm, not a campaign

Businesses that win on reviews are systematic, not charming. The request is a step in the job, sent by text within an hour of a good outcome, using a link that opens straight to the review box. A handful every week beats forty in one push and eleven months of silence.

Respond to every single one

Every review gets a reply, including the four-star ones that say nothing. Replies show buyers a human is home and let you restate what you did and where, in natural language. On a negative review, answer calmly, name what went wrong, and move it to a phone number.

Never buy them, never gate them

Purchased reviews get filtered, and eventually the vendor selling them gets your profile suspended. Gating is subtler and equally prohibited: routing satisfied customers to Google while sending unhappy ones to a private form breaks Google's policy. Ask everyone the same way.

Velocity beats lifetime totals

Owners fixate on the total. Google and buyers both weigh recency. A profile holding 120 reviews with three from the past fortnight usually outperforms one holding 400 whose newest is two years old, because the second implies a business that stopped. Treat reviews per month as the number that matters.

Citations and NAP consistency

A citation is any place online listing your name, address, and phone number. Google cross-references them to decide whether it believes your business exists where you claim. Conflicting information rarely causes a dramatic drop. It quietly caps how far Google will trust the listing, which is worse, because nothing looks broken.

The Miami version is almost always movement: a relocation, a new suite number inside a Brickell tower, a rebrand, or three tracking numbers spread across a website, a directory, and an ad campaign nobody turned off. Every stale copy is a vote against the current one.

Fix the tier that matters and skip the rest. Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, and the two or three vertical directories that dominate your industry carry real weight. Match the format exactly everywhere, down to whether you write Suite or Ste, and keep tracking numbers inside your own site's dynamic replacement. Twenty accurate listings beat five hundred sloppy ones.

On-site local signals

Your site is where relevance gets built at scale, where you prove depth, and where most Miami businesses have the least defensible work.

One real page for every service you sell

Google ranks pages, not companies. A single services page with a paragraph per offering competes against dedicated pages written by people who took the topic seriously. Give every money service its own page: your process, realistic timelines, what moves the price, the Miami-Dade permitting and code realities that apply, photos of your own work, and the questions buyers ask on the phone. Five deep pages outrank twenty thin ones.

Neighborhood relevance without doorway spam

The tempting shortcut is a page for every municipality with the place name swapped. Google has spent years getting better at recognizing exactly that, and mass-produced location pages now drag on a domain rather than lifting it. If the only difference between two of your pages is a neighborhood name, you have built a liability, not coverage.

How to write about an area you actually serve

Real area pages are earned. Write one only where you have substance: jobs finished there, reviews from customers there, photos taken there, and details only somebody who works the area would know. A Doral warehouse conversion carries different code and loading constraints than a Coral Gables historic renovation, and selling to a Sunny Isles condo board is nothing like selling to a Kendall homeowner. If you cannot write four hundred honest words about an area, go earn the material first.

Schema markup for local businesses

Schema is structured data in your page code describing the page in a format built for machines. It will not rank you on its own. It removes ambiguity, and in local search ambiguity is expensive.

Use the most specific type available: Dentist, Plumber, LegalService, HomeAndConstructionBusiness, Restaurant, rather than generic LocalBusiness whenever a subtype exists. Include your exact name, address, and phone in the same format as your citations, plus coordinates, hours, areas served, and a sameAs list pointing at your profile and social accounts. Add Service markup on service pages and FAQPage markup where you answer real questions.

Two rules keep it safe. Never mark up content a visitor cannot see, and never add review markup for reviews that live on Google rather than on your own site, a violation that can strip rich results across the domain.

Spanish-language local SEO

Roughly seven in ten Miami-Dade households speak a language other than English at home, Spanish first. Most competitors still publish in English only, leaving an enormous pool of demand contested by very few businesses. This is the largest structural opportunity in Miami local search, and the one most often botched.

The botched version is machine translation dropped onto duplicate pages. Spanish speakers spot it in one sentence, and search terms do not translate literally anyway. People search the phrase their family uses, which here is shaped by Cuban, Venezuelan, Colombian, and Argentine habits at once. Run Spanish keyword research as its own exercise instead of translating your English list.

Then write natively. Have a fluent writer produce the Spanish pages as original copy, keep them on their own URLs, and connect the versions with hreflang so Google serves the right one instead of treating them as duplicates. Extend it past the website: reply to Spanish reviews in Spanish, and answer profile questions in both. In parts of the county, Haitian Creole deserves the same consideration.

Done properly, you are competing in a lane most of your market never entered.

Tracking that tells you something

Local reporting fails predictably: it counts what is easy to count. A single ranking number is close to meaningless here, because your position changes with every block the searcher moves. Track four things instead.

  • Calls, with recordings. The lead here is usually a phone call. Use dynamic number insertion so calls can be attributed to organic search, and read your profile's call metrics separately.
  • Direction requests. For anywhere customers visit, this is the closest thing to a purchase-intent signal Google gives you for free, and it usually moves before revenue does.
  • Rank grids, not rank numbers. Geo grid tools check your position from dozens of points across a map and return a heat map. That is the only honest picture of a service area.
  • Organic leads and booked jobs. Rankings are a leading indicator. Revenue is the verdict. Anything you cannot tie back to a customer is decoration.

Record a baseline first, or you lose the ability to prove progress later.

Questions, answered

How long does local SEO take in Miami?

Profile and citation corrections often show movement inside four to eight weeks. Competitive Miami categories such as law, dental, and home services usually need three to six months before map pack positions shift meaningfully, and longer to hold them. Established domains with real review volume move faster than brand new ones.

Do I need a physical address to rank in Miami?

You need a real address to verify, but you can hide it. Google supports service-area businesses that travel to the customer, including contractors, mobile services, and home care. Verify at your genuine location, hide it publicly, and define the cities you cover. Virtual offices and mailbox rentals are not eligible and cause suspensions in Miami-Dade.

Can one business rank across multiple Miami neighborhoods?

Yes, but not evenly. Distance from the searcher is a factor you cannot override, so one location ranks strongest near itself and fades as the searcher moves away. You widen that radius with review velocity, genuine area pages backed by real work, and local links. Expect strong positions near your address, not identical results county wide.

Should my reviews mention services and neighborhoods?

Naturally worded mentions help, because review text feeds how Google understands what you do and where. Never script the wording and never offer anything in exchange. What works is a request phrased as an open question, asking which service you handled and how the job went, so customers add the detail themselves.
